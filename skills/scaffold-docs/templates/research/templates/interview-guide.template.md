# Interview Guide: <Study Name>

*See `user-research.md` for the findings write-up afterward.*

## Research goal
What are we trying to learn from these interviews?

- State the goal as a question the interviews should be able to answer — if you can't picture what a good answer looks like, the core questions below won't be well-aimed either.
- Note the decision this feeds, even loosely — it keeps the question set focused on what's actually actionable instead of general curiosity.

## Screener criteria
Who qualifies as a participant, and how are they recruited?

- Write criteria specific enough that two different recruiters would select the same people — "active users" is too loose; "used the product at least 3 times in the past 30 days" is screenable.
- Include disqualifying criteria too (competitors' employees, people who've participated in research recently) — these prevent a skewed or contaminated sample as much as the inclusion criteria do.

## Warm-up questions
Low-stakes questions to open the conversation and build rapport.

- Keep these easy to answer and unrelated to anything you need to be careful not to bias — the goal is comfort, not data.
- Avoid leading into the core topic too early; a warm-up question that hints at "the thing we're really asking about" can prime answers before the core questions get there.

## Core questions
The main question set, ordered from broad to specific. Note any probes/follow-ups for likely answers.

- Order broad-to-specific so participants describe their own experience before you narrow to the behavior you're investigating — asking the narrow question first anchors their answer to your framing.
- Write open-ended questions ("walk me through the last time you...") rather than yes/no or leading ones ("don't you find X frustrating?") — the latter tells participants the answer you want.
- Add probes for likely answers ("if they say X, ask...") so the interviewer isn't improvising follow-ups live — that's what keeps different interviewers' sessions comparable.

*Ask about specific past behavior, not hypothetical future behavior — "what would you do if..." reliably gets optimistic answers that don't predict real behavior.*

## Wrap-up
Closing questions, and how participants are thanked/compensated.

- Leave room for a catch-all ("anything we didn't ask that you expected to?") — it regularly surfaces the most useful finding of the session.
- State compensation and thanks explicitly so every interviewer handles it the same way across sessions.

## Logistics
Session length, recording consent, and who's note-taking vs. facilitating.

- Confirm recording consent is asked and logged before the session starts, not assumed — this is what makes quoting participants in the resulting `user-research.md` usable.
- Assign facilitator and note-taker roles ahead of time; a facilitator who's also trying to type detailed notes tends to do both jobs worse.
- This guide is meant to be used as-is for this round of interviews — if the research goal changes materially, write a new guide rather than editing this one mid-study so past sessions stay comparable to later ones.
