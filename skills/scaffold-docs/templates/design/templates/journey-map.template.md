# Journey Map: <Journey Name>

## Scope
What journey is being mapped, and for which persona/segment?

- Name the specific persona or segment this journey represents — a journey map for "the user" in general tends to average away the friction any one real segment actually feels.
- State where the journey starts and ends explicitly (first awareness? first purchase? renewal?) — without a stated boundary, scope tends to expand mid-mapping.
- If this segment's journey differs meaningfully from another segment's, map them separately rather than merging into one map that fits neither well.

## Stages
| Stage | User goal | Touchpoints | Actions | Emotional state |
|---|---|---|---|---|

- "User goal" should be the user's goal, not the business's — a stage's goal is "understand if this will work for me," not "view the pricing page."
- "Touchpoints" covers every surface involved, not just yours — support emails, third-party review sites, and word of mouth are touchpoints too, even if you don't control them.
- "Emotional state" is only useful if it's backed by something observed (research, support tickets, session recordings) — an invented emotional state is just decoration on the map.

## Pain points
Where does friction or frustration peak across this journey?

- Tie each pain point to a specific stage and, ideally, to evidence (a drop-off in analytics, a recurring support complaint, a research quote) rather than an assumption.
- Distinguish pain points caused by the product from ones caused by the underlying process (e.g. a slow third-party approval) — the fix differs, and conflating them misdirects effort.

## Opportunities
Where are the highest-leverage points to improve this journey?

- Prioritize opportunities against the pain points above, not independently — an opportunity that doesn't map to a named pain point needs its own justification for why it matters.
- Call out opportunities that require cross-team or cross-surface changes explicitly, since those are the ones most likely to stall without an owner.

## Related flows
Link to the `flow-spec.md` files that cover the individual interactions within this journey in detail.

- Link at the stage level where possible, so a reader can go from "this stage has friction" straight to the flow spec that covers the exact steps.
- If a stage has no flow spec yet, note that as a gap rather than leaving the link out silently.
