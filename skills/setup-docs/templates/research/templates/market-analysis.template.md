# Market Analysis: <Market/Segment>

## Market definition
What market/segment is being sized, and what's explicitly excluded?

- Draw the boundary tightly enough that two people reading this would classify the same company the same way — "the CRM market" is looser than "CRM software for B2B sales teams under 50 people."
- State what's explicitly excluded (adjacent categories, geographies, customer sizes) — the exclusions are often what keep a TAM estimate honest instead of inflated.
- If a top-down (industry report) and bottom-up (unit economics × addressable count) definition would draw the boundary differently, say which one you're using here and why.

## Size & growth
TAM/SAM/SOM estimates, growth rate, and the sources/assumptions behind each number.

- Show the calculation, not just the final number — a bottom-up estimate (unit economics × addressable population) should show its inputs so a reader can sanity-check or challenge them.
- Cite a source or explicit assumption for every number; an unsourced market size is just a guess wearing a spreadsheet.
- Distinguish TAM (total addressable), SAM (serviceable, given your business model/geography), and SOM (realistically obtainable) — collapsing them into one number overstates the actual opportunity.

## Trends
What's shifting in this market that changes the opportunity (technology, regulation, buyer behavior)?

- Focus on trends that change the size or shape of the opportunity, not just "the market is growing" — a trend should imply something about timing or strategy.
- Distinguish trends you have evidence for (data, multiple independent sources) from ones that are your own read of the room — flag the latter as such.

## Target segment
Which slice of this market are we actually pursuing, and why?

- Name the specific segment, not the whole market defined above — this is the SAM/SOM made concrete: who you'd actually sell to first.
- Explain why this segment first — underserved by incumbents, easiest to reach, best unit economics, fastest feedback loop — rather than leaving it as an assertion.

## Competitive landscape
Summary only — link to a `competitive-analysis.md` if one exists for this market.

- Keep this to a few sentences on where competitors sit relative to the target segment above — the full breakdown belongs in a dedicated competitive-analysis, not duplicated here.
- If no competitive-analysis exists yet and this market has real incumbents, flag that as a gap rather than skipping the section.

## Opportunity & risks
Where's the opening, and what could close it (new entrant, platform shift, regulation)?

- State the opening in terms of the target segment and trends above, not the market in the abstract — "the opening" should follow from what you've already established.
- List risks that could shrink SOM or delay the window, not just generic market risk — a regulation change or platform shift that closes the opening is different from ordinary competitive pressure.

## Methodology caveats
Note anything that makes these numbers soft — small sample, dated sources, self-reported data.

- Be specific about which numbers are soft and why, not a blanket disclaimer — "the SAM estimate relies on a 2022 industry report" tells a reader more than "estimates are approximate."
- This is a write-once snapshot of the market as understood at the date above — if the market shifts materially, that's a new market-analysis, not an edit to this one.
