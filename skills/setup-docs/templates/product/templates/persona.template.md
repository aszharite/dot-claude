# Persona: <Persona Name>

## Summary
One or two sentences: who is this, in context.

- Anchor it in a role or situation, not a demographic — "a support lead triaging 50+ tickets a day," not "a 35-year-old professional."
- This is the sentence someone skims when deciding which persona applies to their feature — make it specific enough to rule personas in or out.

## Goals
What is this person trying to achieve when they use the product?

- Distinguish the goal from the task — "close tickets faster" is a goal; "use the bulk-close button" is a task that might serve it.
- If every persona for this product has the same goals, they're probably not different personas — find what's actually distinct about this one.

## Pain points
What frustrates them today, with or without our product?

- Include friction that exists even without your product — competitor gaps, workarounds, manual steps — not just complaints about your current UI.
- Tie each pain point to evidence if you can; an unverified pain point is a guess about what to build.

## Context & behavior
How, when, and where do they encounter the product? Technical proficiency, constraints, environment.

- Cover the environment constraints that shape design decisions: device, network, time pressure, whether they're multitasking or focused.
- Technical proficiency isn't just "novice/expert" — note what they're expert *at*, since domain expertise and product familiarity are different axes.

## Representative quote
A real or composited quote that captures how this persona thinks/talks — keeps the persona from becoming an abstraction.

- Pull from actual interview transcripts or support tickets where possible — a quote invented to sound plausible defeats the purpose.
- *A good test: if this quote could belong to any persona, it's too generic — it should sound like this specific person.*

## Evidence
Link to the `user-research.md` or interviews this persona is grounded in — a persona without evidence is a guess wearing a name tag.

- Note how many people this persona is drawn from and over what period — a persona built from two conversations six months apart carries less weight than one shaped by an ongoing study.
- If this persona hasn't been validated with real research yet, say so explicitly instead of letting it read as settled fact.
